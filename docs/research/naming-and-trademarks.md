## 2. Godot Engine Trademark Policy

The Godot Foundation manages and enforces trademarks for "GODOT", "GODOT ENGINE", and the Godot robot logo ([Godot Foundation Trademark Policy and License](https://godot.foundation/policies-and-procedures/trademark-policy/)). The engine source code is distributed under the MIT license ([Godot Engine License](https://godotengine.org/license/)).

### What Is Allowed for Third-Party Addons and Apps
* **Automatic Compatibility License**: The Godot Foundation grants a non-exclusive, royalty-free, worldwide license to use the "GODOT" and "GODOT ENGINE" word marks to convey compatibility for products and services associated with the engine (such as addons, plugins, tools, and companion services).
* **Descriptive Phrasing**: Third-party projects may state that they are "made for the Godot Engine", "for Godot", or "compatible with Godot".
* **Games and Runtimes**: Games made using Godot may display the Godot logo in splash screens and in-game credits ([Godot Press Kit Guidelines](https://godotengine.org/press/)).

### What Is Not Allowed
* **Logo Misappropriation**: Third parties cannot use the Godot robot logo (or any confusing derivative) as the primary icon, avatar, or logo of their own project, store listing, company, or website.
* **Implying Official Endorsement**: The trademark cannot be used in isolation or in a manner that falsely suggests the software is created, authorized, or officially distributed by the Godot Foundation or core team.
* **Trademark and Domain Registrations**: Third parties cannot register domain names or trademarks incorporating "Godot" or "Godot Engine".
* **Logo Alteration**: If the Godot logo is displayed in context, official unmodified assets must be used without stretching, coloring distortions, or alterations.

## 3. Unity Trademark Guidelines

Unity Technologies enforces specific branding guidelines for third parties referencing the Unity platform ([Unity Guidelines for Using Trademarks](https://unity.com/legal/trademarks) and [Unity Brand Guidelines](https://unity.com/legal/brand-guidelines)).

### Permitted Descriptive Use
* **Adjectival Use for Compatibility**: Third-party developers may refer to Unity word marks strictly to describe factual compatibility or intended platform integration (e.g., "Handview for Unity" or "compatible with the Unity editor").
* **Word Order Requirement**: The third-party project name must precede any reference to Unity. "Handview for Unity" is acceptable; "Unity Handview" or "Unity Remote View" is prohibited.

### Prohibited Practices
* **No Logo Use Without License**: Third parties cannot use the official Unity corporate logo, cube mark, or stylized logotype on store icons, splash screens, banners, or promotional images without an explicit partner agreement.
* **No False Association**: Naming and presentation must not suggest that Unity Technologies created, sponsors, or maintains the software.
* **No Premature Feature Claims**: Marketing an app as supporting Unity before the integration actually works violates store guidelines on truthful representation.

### Mandatory Legal Attribution and Disclaimer
Any website, store listing, or documentation that mentions Unity should include the standard attribution notice:
> "Unity is a trademark or registered trademark of Unity Technologies or its affiliates in the U.S. and elsewhere."

Store listings should also include an independent disclaimer:
> "This project is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by Unity Technologies."

## 4. Google Play Store Policies

Google Play strictly regulates metadata, app naming, and intellectual property to prevent consumer deception ([Google Play Developer Policy Center](https://play.google.com/about/developer-content-policy/)).

### Metadata Character Limits and Structure
* **App Title Limit**: Maximum 30 characters ([Google Play Metadata Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898684)).
* **Short Description Limit**: Maximum 80 characters.
* **No Separate Subtitle Field**: Unlike the Apple App Store (which provides a dedicated 30-character Subtitle field), Google Play only provides App Title (30 chars), Short Description (80 chars), and Full Description (4000 chars).
* **Length Check on Proposed Subtitle**: The planned text `"Handview - Game View remote for Godot and Unity"` contains 47 characters. It cannot fit into the Google Play Title field.

### Impersonation and Third-Party Trademarks
* **Impersonation Rules**: Apps may not use titles, icons, or developer names that mislead users into thinking the app is published by or officially affiliated with another company or project ([Google Play Impersonation Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888379)).
* **Third-Party Trademarks in Titles**:
  * Using brand names as the primary title element is prohibited.
  * Mentioning multiple major third-party trademarks inside a 30-character title (e.g., "Handview: Godot & Unity") frequently triggers automated review rejections for keyword spam or impersonation.
  * The approved naming structure for companion tools on Android is: `[Independent Brand Name] for [Platform]`.
* **Intellectual Property Enforcement**: Using third-party brand logos (Godot mascot, Unity cube) in app icons or feature graphics leads to immediate takedowns ([Google Play Intellectual Property Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10149488)).
* **Deceptive Functionality**: Promoting features that are not yet functional (e.g., advertising Unity support in store metadata while the feature is only planned for a future release) violates Google Play's Deceptive Behavior policy.

## 5. Recommendation for Handview

### Concrete DOs
* **Own the Primary Brand**: Keep "Handview" as the distinct primary product name in all listings, titles, and repository documentation.
* **Keep the Play Store Title within 30 Characters**: Structure the title as `Handview - Game View Remote` (29 characters) or `Handview: Remote Game View` (27 characters).
* **Use the Short Description for Compatibility**: Place the descriptive engine references inside the 80-character Short Description where there is sufficient space: `Remote game view streamer for Godot Engine and PC game editors.` (63 characters).
* **Phase Platform Claims Honestly**: Until the Unity integration is built, tested, and shipped in the APK, reference only Godot in store metadata. Introduce Unity into descriptions only when the feature is active.
* **Design an Original Icon**: Use custom brand iconography (e.g., a hand, screen, or stream concept) without embedding the Godot robot mascot or Unity cube.
* **Include Disclaimers in Full Description**: Add explicit legal footers at the bottom of the Play Store listing and GitHub README:
  * "Godot is a trademark of the Godot Foundation. Handview is an independent third-party tool and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Godot Foundation."
  * "Unity is a trademark or registered trademark of Unity Technologies or its affiliates. Handview is an independent tool and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Unity Technologies."

### Concrete DO NOTs
* **DO NOT** use the planned subtitle `"Handview - Game View remote for Godot and Unity"` as the App Title (exceeds the 30-character limit at 47 characters).
* **DO NOT** use `"Godot Remote"`, `"Unity Remote"`, or put `"Godot"` or `"Unity"` as the first word of the app title.
* **DO NOT** include the Godot mascot head or the Unity cube logo inside the Android app icon, feature graphic, or splash screens.
* **DO NOT** mention Unity in the Play Store title or short description while the app only supports Godot.
* **DO NOT** attempt to register trademarks or web domains containing "Godot" or "Unity".
